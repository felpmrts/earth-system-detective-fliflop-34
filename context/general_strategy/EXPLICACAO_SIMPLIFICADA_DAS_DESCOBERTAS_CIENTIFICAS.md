**1. What is our Scientific Discovery System?**
It is a **physical auditing and discovery engine** coupled to NASA satellite and climate reanalysis data<sup></sup>. Instead of feeding raw data into an Artificial Intelligence "black box"<sup></sup>, it operates in three stages:

- **Isolates the real signal from the annual cycle:** Removes the obvious effect of seasons (summer/winter) and separates slow multi-decade trends from short-term extreme shocks<sup></sup>.
- **Tests hypotheses without "peeking into the future":** Splits the 25 years of data (`2001–2025`) into chronologically separated blocks and requires any model or climate precursor to beat simple river inertia (*"predicting next month will look like this month"*)<sup></sup>.
- **Detects regime breaks in real time:** Automatically identifies when the historical relationship between rainfall, heat, soil moisture, and river discharge shifts pattern — working both in deep analyses and instantaneously ( $< 50\text{ ms}$ ) when the user clicks any point on the map.

**2. What did it discover in the 2001–2025 data?**

- **More sunlight in the Amazon from cloud retreat vs. smoke-driven solar blackout in 2024:** In the **Madeira** and **Tapajós** basins, solar radiation reaching the ground rose consistently over 25 years<sup></sup>. The data show this long-term trend came from **declining cloud cover**, not a cleaner atmosphere<sup></sup>. At the same time, looking at cloud-free clear-sky radiation, the detector isolated an extreme negative shock in **September 2024** ( $D_M = 19.69$ in the Madeira) — the exact signature of continental smoke plumes blocking sunlight<sup></sup>.
- **Accelerating air drying and regime shifts across basins:** By accounting for daily heat peaks, the system revealed that air drying (`VPD`) in the **Paraná** and **São Francisco** basins grew roughly **23% to 26% faster** than traditional estimates based only on monthly mean temperature. Moreover, the detector flagged structural shifts in the climate-to-discharge relationship in the **São Francisco (2014)** and **Paraná (2017)** — reflecting the combined effect of megadroughts and large-reservoir management — while the **Guaíba (RS)** maintained a rapid, direct response to extreme rainfall, as in the historic **May 2024** flood<sup></sup>.
- **Continental climate seesaw and 19 filtered oceanic precursors:** The system confirmed a phase opposition ("seesaw") in solar radiation between the **South (Guaíba)** and the **Center-North (São Francisco and Tapajós)**<sup></sup>. It also screened over a thousand oceanic combinations and isolated **19 statistically significant connections** (with up to 6 months lead time) that survive even after discounting the river's own memory and — for Atlantic connections — the simultaneous effect of Pacific *El Niño*.
- **Why lean mathematical models beat heavy AI algorithms:** Faced with unprecedented climate extremes (2020–2024), linear models and penalized smooth curves (`RidgeCV` and `SplineGAM`) systematically outperformed decision trees and *Random Forests*<sup></sup>. Whereas tree models "flatten out" and fail when encountering droughts or floods outside the historical range, continuous models extrapolate the physical trend without collapsing<sup></sup>. The system also automatically rejected the *Murray-Darling* basin (Australia) as soon as it detected the absence of a valid continuous series in the official GloFAS API, refusing to run scientific discovery on fallback synthetic data.
